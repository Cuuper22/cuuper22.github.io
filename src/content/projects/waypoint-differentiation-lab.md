Waypoint turns a lesson map and a pseudonymized learner profile into tomorrow's classroom supports, with receipts attached to every recommendation. The handout, the receipts, the audit, and the quality report all generate from the same packet data, so the thing a teacher hands a student and the thing that justifies it can never quietly drift apart.

It's built on a compact-first MCP. Default tool payloads stay small while the full evidence is one call away, and even the built-in prompt is budgeted, so the default hand-off is a route through tools rather than another wall of text. Every recommendation preserves the standard it maps to (`RI.7.2`), maps to UDL, avoids student-facing labels, and carries a progress check. The domain constraints aren't decoration. They're the difference between real differentiation and generic worksheet generation.

The live site has a three-act reviewer walkthrough: packet preview, a Receipts Rail, the quality gate, and a five-minute review scorecard, all in one browser pass. The whole design assumes a skeptical reviewer with five minutes and no patience for black boxes, which is the correct assumption for anything that touches a kid's IEP.
